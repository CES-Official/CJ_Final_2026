Technology Should Serve Children, Not the Other Way Around

Helping children develop a healthy and thoughtful relationship with technology.

Technology is becoming a bigger part of childhood. Children use it to learn, communicate, find information, create, navigate the world and connect with others. And as AI becomes part of everyday life, technology will become even more deeply woven into how children learn and work.

But there is a bigger question for parents and schools: Are children learning to use technology, or is technology beginning to shape how they think, focus and make decisions?

26 years

Dr. Sahar Yousef's UC Berkeley course Mind, Machine, and Meaning: Living Intentionally in the Digital Age highlights a striking projection: an average 18-year-old in the United States could spend 93% of their remaining free time looking at screens. Assuming a 90-year life expectancy, that amounts to 26 years.

The point is not that technology is bad. In fact, technology can help us communicate, learn, create and accomplish things that were once difficult or impossible. The challenge is developing a healthy and thoughtful relationship with it.

Children cannot simply opt out of technology

For today's children, avoiding technology altogether is neither practical nor necessary. The more important goal is to help them understand how to use technology without allowing technology to make every decision for them. That means learning to ask:

  • Should I believe this?
  • Should I share this?
  • Should I click this?
  • Should I give this app my information?
  • Should I accept this AI answer?
  • Should I let technology do this thinking for me?

These are not just technology questions. They are questions of judgment.

AI makes this even more important

AI is not simply another digital tool. It can help children explore ideas, understand difficult concepts, create and solve problems. But it can also make it easier to accept information without checking it, rely on technology without understanding it, or allow a machine to do thinking that the student should be doing.

Dr. Yousef's course specifically examines the relationship between AI, technology, human cognition and critical thinking, including how people can use AI while maintaining human judgment. For children, that means developing a simple but powerful habit:

PauseQuestionEvaluateDecide
  • Before accepting an AI answer: Is it correct?
  • Before sharing something online: Is it reliable?
  • Before giving an app personal information: Do I really need to share it?
  • Before using AI for an assignment: Am I using it to help me learn, or am I letting it do the learning for me?

These are the habits that help children remain active participants rather than passive users of technology.

This is where digital readiness matters

Digital readiness is not simply about knowing how to use a computer, app or AI tool. Technology will change. Today's tools will be replaced by tomorrow's tools. What children need are skills that remain useful even when the technology changes.

Understand AI

Know what AI can do, where it can make mistakes, and how to use it responsibly.

Evaluate Information

Recognize misinformation, question sources, and think critically about what they see online.

Stay Safe

Understand privacy, digital identity, manipulation, and responsible online behavior.

Think Independently

Evaluate evidence, solve problems, and make decisions rather than simply accepting the first answer technology provides.

This is the idea behind COMPUDON Junior

COMPUDON Junior does not teach children to fear technology. It teaches them to use it thoughtfully. For students aged 8–14, COMPUDON Junior brings together AI Awareness, Media Literacy, Cyber Safety and Critical Thinking in one structured program.

The goal is not to produce children who know how to use every new technology. It is to develop children who know when to use technology, how to use it, when to question it, and when to stop and think for themselves.

Because the technology will keep changing, the ability to think well will matter even more.

Think First.
