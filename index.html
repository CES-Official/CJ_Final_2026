Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions, answered

What is COMPUDON Junior?

COMPUDON Junior is India's National Championship on AI Awareness, Media Literacy, Cyber Safety & Critical Thinking for school students aged 8–14. It combines a structured study program with a benchmarked assessment and a national recognition pathway, operated by CyberLearning Educational Society (CES) and powered by a Microsoft Global Training Partner.

What is the eligibility criteria for participation?

Students aged 8 to 14 years as of August 15, 2026, are eligible to participate in COMPUDON Junior Season VII. Eligible participants are typically studying in Grades 3 to 9, with age-appropriate learning resources and assessments tailored to each age group.

Is COMPUDON Junior aligned with CBSE requirements?

Yes. The program is mapped to NEP 2020 and supports schools in delivering CBSE Circular Skill-81/2025 and Acad-39/2026, with ready-made documentation for compliance and future-readiness records.

What does Season VII cost?

Season VII participation is ₹699 per student, all inclusive.

How much teacher effort does the program require?

Very little. COMPUDON Junior provides the study materials, assessment platform, student guides, and detailed performance reports. Teachers simply facilitate participation and encourage students. There is no need to develop lesson plans, prepare question papers, or evaluate assessments. Schools may also choose to have parents register eligible students directly, further reducing the administrative effort for teachers.

Prashast Prajapati — Sanskaar Valley School, Bhopal

I still remember my first attempt at COMPUDON Junior championship. I'd made the merit list, but there was no trophy or medal. At the time, it felt like the end of the world.

But I came back the next year. And the year after that.

Over time, COMPUDON Junior stopped being just another competition for me. It became the place where I discovered I was good at technology. Every challenge taught me something new, pushed me to think differently, and gave me more confidence. Long before I was building projects or writing real code, it made me believe in myself.

Today, I'm in Grade 11, preparing for JEE with the goal of studying Computer Science at IIT. Since those early competitions, I've earned 10 Microsoft certifications, built several technology projects, and won multiple national-level technology competitions. Looking back, I can honestly say that my journey began with COMPUDON.

If I had let that first disappointment stop me, I would have missed everything that came after.

So, if you're thinking about participating, take the chance. You might win a trophy. You might not.

Either way, you'll learn a lot of new things, you will challenge yourself, meet other students who share your passion for technology, and discover you're capable of much more than you think.

One attempt may not change your life, but it could be the first step toward something much bigger.

It certainly was for me.
